## America's Mental Health Crisis

It's no secret that America's facing a major mental health crisis affecting all of the country's numerous demographics. In such a diverse country, the impetuses for this
crisis are as numerous as the country's demographics, and thus trying to address all of them in one place is a nigh impossible task. Therefore, this post won't attempt to
do so, but it will address several impetuses I find pertinent, their origins, examples of them, and potential solutions to these issues.

## The Crisis

## Prophecy Fulfilled?

For those of us who read the scriptures and have been blessed to understand what we're reading to a certain degree, nothing discussed so far should be surprising, as the 
prophets foretold of this time. 

In Daniel chapter 2, Daniel interprets a dream Nebuchadnezzar II, king of Babylon, had, in which he saw a statue with its head made out of gold, torso made out of silver,
stomach and thighs made out of brass, the rest of its legs made out of iron, and feet made out of iron mixed with clay have its feet destroyed by a stone "cut out without
hands" (Dan 2:34, KJV).

Daniel explains to Nebuchadnezzar II that each of the four parts of the statue (the legs and feet are one part) represent different kingdoms here on Earth, with the
Babylonian Empire under Nebuchadnezzar II being the first, represented by the golden head. Then, after the Babylonians, another kingdom will arise, represented by the 
silver torso. After that kingdom, another will arise, represented by the brass belly. Following the pattern, the fourth kingdom, represented by the iron mixed with clay,
will arise. 

Rudimentary research into the subject or history reveals the identity of the remaining kingdoms. As the scriptures also prophesied, the Medo-Persian Empire under the
command of Cyrus conquered the Babylonian Empire, making them the silver torso. Then, the Macedonian Greeks under Alexander conquered the Persians, making them the brass
belly. Finally, the Romans under Lucius Aemilius Paulus defeated the Macedonians at the Battle of Pydna, making them the fourth kingdom. At least, this is the consensus
interpretation.

In my view, the above interpretation is not incorrect, but **incomplete**. What people miss is that the fourth kingdom did not end with Rome's fall. In Revelation chapter
13, John depicts the fourth kingdom as a beast with seven heads, with one head being wounded to death. But, rather than dying, the wound healed and the beast gained power
again. While there is no Roman Empire in name anymore, it still exists in spirit as the main influence of what we call "western civilization," even more than any alleged
"Judeo-Christian" values. The structure of western society, from government, to legal codes, to philosophy, to religion, to the organization of society, to infrastructure,
all the way down to our calendar have conspicuous Roman influence. As such, we're **still living** in Daniel's fourth kingdom and thus studying Daniel's prophecies will
provide insight into the world we live in today.

The operative verse for this blog post is Daniel 2:42, which states: 

> And as the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken.

"Partly strong, and partly broken" is an apropos description of western civilization. It's strong, as its the dominant culture in the world and imposes itself on those that
come into contact with it, either through forced or voluntary assimilation. See it for yourself outside. The descendants of immigrants from the Middle East, Eastern Asia,
India, or Africa discard many customs from their homelands in favor of western customs. They dress like westerners, unless they come from a strict Muslim home, they conform
to western relationship paradigms, and find jobs in the western corporate culture. 

But the weakness, the clay in the statue's feet, comes from embracing those wicked philosophies. Western society inundates us with deleterious ideas that we accept because
we're never informed of the alternatives. We know there's an illness, but the ailment remains mysterious because in its shrewdness, the illness blinds people to its origin.
